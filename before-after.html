The answers were never the problem.

A hands-on walkthrough of the live build, then a second review against Magna's own annotated screenshots. Both passes reached the same conclusion: the model's output is good and the surface around it is not. The construction-risk answer was specific, sourced and well structured — and then the app rendered LaTeX as raw source, drew a flowchart in keyboard characters, and gave a user no way to copy any of it. Every pair below is one defect in the live build against the same moment in the redesigned prototypes.

§2b

The four that block launch

The first audit tested the shell with prose questions, so it missed this entirely. The moment someone asks a maths, code or diagram question, the answer renderer fails — not degrades, fails. These four make whole categories of seat unable to use the product at all.

R1 · LATEX

Mathematics is never parsed

An integral-calculus question returns about 25 consecutive lines of literal source. Every technical seat — finance, engineering, planning — is unusable until this ships.

R2 · DIAGRAMS

Flowcharts degrade to ASCII

The model offers "a visual flowchart and a text version", then draws both in box-drawing characters. There is no Mermaid or SVG renderer behind it.

R3 · CODE

Code blocks have no affordances

A bare dark rectangle. No language label, no filename, no wrap toggle — and no copy button, the most-pressed control in every chat product on earth.

R4 · COPY

Nothing copies a whole answer

Getting a response out of MagnaVERSE and into a board pack means dragging a manual selection across a scrolling region, and losing the formatting on the way.

01

A global header nobody could name

S1High

What was wrong

A permanent app-wide bar holding seven unrelated controls, five of them unlabelled glyphs with no tooltips. Two of the four sovereignty tiers in its dropdown are dead ("coming soon"). Neither ChatGPT nor Claude ships an app-wide header, and the audit found nothing in this one that could not live in the sidebar or in context.

What changed

Deleted. Everything global moved into the sidebar where it is labelled and always in the same place; everything per-thread moved into a slim thread bar. The command palette keeps its ⌘K hint instead of hiding behind a bare magnifier.

Why it matters: the header was consuming the top of every screen to show controls nobody could name, and hiding the app's best feature — the command palette — behind an unlabelled icon. Removing it costs a week and gives the answer the whole window.

02

One orphaned Share icon, and no way to copy

S2 · R4Launch blocker

What was wrong

Three unlabelled icons appear on some messages — Share, a recalled-memory readout that prints the raw key memories:context/interest-learning-coding with a relevance score, and a book. There is no copy button anywhere, and nothing at all that copies a whole answer.

What changed

A single consistent row on every assistant message. Copy and Export do real work; the raw memory key and the relevance score are gone from the end-user surface. The transcript scrolls inside its own region, so the row stays where you expect it.

Why it matters: every answer this product generates has to leave it — into a board pack, an email, a ticket, a change order. Today that journey starts with dragging a selection across a moving scroll region and losing the formatting. This is the cheapest of the four launch blockers to fix and the one users hit most often.

03

A 3D robot standing on the send button

C1 · S5Critical

What was wrong

The 3D companion is rendered over the composer's model selector and send control. The bell menu offers ten alternatives — Fox, Wolf, Husky, Shiba Inu, Alpaca, Horse, Cow, Bull, Deer, Stag — and a "hide companion" item, none of which changes the fact that the default state occludes the primary action of the product.

What changed

Removed from all six prototypes. Nothing floats over the composer. Where the app needs a mascot moment, the animated Magna mark does it — as the thinking indicator, with honest status text that changes as the turn progresses.

Why it matters: this is the first thing a demo audience notices, and it is a one-line fix. Until it is gone, every live demonstration of MagnaVERSE opens with a misclick.

04

Developer telemetry above every answer

S9 · C4 · H3High

What was wrong

Roughly 200 px of trace sits expanded above every answer, pushing the answer below the fold — the audit had to scroll to read every single response. The trace panel is a deliberate product surface, but what it shows is staging exhaust: raw step names, a monthly budget of $5,000 and per-request costs visible to every seat.

What changed

Collapsed to a single line that says something true and useful — how long it thought, how many tools it ran, how many documents it searched. Expanding it gives a plain-language breakdown. The Langfuse internals stay in the observability tool where they belong.

Why it matters: the answer is what people came for; it should be the first thing on screen. And a product that prints "Final step completed without captured I/O." at a customer reads as unfinished, whatever the answer underneath it is worth.

05

"Latest Kimi" is not a model name

S6 · M5High

What was wrong

"Latest Kimi" carries no weights, no version and no size. A buyer evaluating a sovereign stack cannot tell what they are being offered, and a user cannot tell why they would switch. The one differentiating fact — that these run on Magna's own hardware — is compressed into the word "private" repeated four times.

What changed

Kimi K2 Instruct · 1T MoE, DeepSeek V3.1 · 671B MoE, Qwen 3 · 235B, NVIDIA Nemotron · 120B — each with a one-line "use this when", and a footer stating the cluster and the GPU count.

Why it matters: sovereignty is the reason to buy MagnaVERSE. The model list is exactly where that claim is either substantiated with specifics or waved at. Right now it is waved at.

06

Code you cannot copy

R3Launch blocker

What was wrong

Nothing identifies the language, nothing names the file, nothing wraps long lines, and nothing copies the block. Body text renders over the default skyline wallpaper at 50% overlay, so contrast fails in the lower third of the viewport — there are eight sliders to tune the photograph and no plain-background option.

What changed

Every code block carries a header and real controls, on a token-driven solid surface. The wallpaper is gone entirely; theme switching is pure CSS custom properties, so it is instant instead of stalling on a raster reload.

Why it matters: an engineering seat cannot use a chat product they cannot copy code out of. This is table stakes in every competing tool, and its absence is the single clearest signal that the answer surface has not been finished.

07

A "visual flowchart" drawn in keyboard characters

R2Launch blocker

What was wrong

There is no Mermaid or SVG renderer, so the "visual" version is byte-for-byte the same as the text version. It cannot be exported, cannot be dropped into a deck, and breaks the moment the window is resized or the answer is copied into anything proportional.

What changed

Diagrams render as SVG and open in a panel beside the chat with Preview and Code tabs. Copy source hands back the Mermaid; Export SVG hands back a vector file that goes straight into a slide.

Why it matters: process, approval-chain and architecture questions are a large share of what an operational brain gets asked. Today every one of them comes back as ASCII art that cannot leave the window.

08

Twenty-five lines of raw LaTeX

R1Launch blocker

What was wrong

No math renderer at all. Every quantitative answer arrives as source. The delimiters are malformed too — $\int x^3 , dx$ uses a bare comma where \, was intended — so shipping a renderer alone will not fix it; the prompt template needs correcting in the same change.

What changed

Maths renders as notation, inline and in display blocks, hand-built in HTML and CSS with no external dependency. The block scrolls inside its own container on a phone rather than forcing the page sideways.

Why it matters: this is the defect that most narrows who can buy. Any seat that asks a quantitative question — commercial, finance, planning, engineering — gets an unusable answer today, and there is no workaround available to the user.

09

Sovereignty, stated before you sign in

H6Medium

What was wrong

Nothing in the live app tells a user where their answer ran, and nothing warns them when a thread containing internal documents moves to the Public tier. For a sovereign deployment, "did this come from our data, and did it stay in the Kingdom?" is the question that matters most, and the product does not answer it.

What changed

The claim is made concrete at every step: named cluster and region before sign-in, a pre-flight that shows public egress blocked, tiers that explain their trade-off, a guardrail that names the exact at-risk files before switching to Public, and a per-answer residency receipt.

Why it matters: Magna is not selling a chat product; it is selling a chat product that runs in Riyadh. Every screen that fails to say so is a screen where the competitor's cheaper hosted model looks identical.

10

Connecting a mail server, in four steps

S10High

What was wrong

MCP is one of the platform's genuine strengths and it is effectively undiscoverable. The one surface that exposes it — an 18-item skills checkbox list — shows internal names like heartbeat and jira-ticket-definer, with every skill on by default. Users are asked to make a decision they have no way to evaluate.

What changed

A catalogue with a four-step wizard, plus typing / in the composer to open a filterable menu of 18 skills and 4 MCP servers, each with a plain-language description. Arrow keys navigate, Enter inserts, Escape returns focus to the composer.

Why it matters: connectors are what turn a chat window into an operational brain. Every system a customer does not connect is a use case they never experience, and today the path to connecting one is invisible.

11

Skills described in the user's language

M4 · C4High

What was wrong

Eighteen checkboxes labelled with repository slugs and cut-off descriptions, all pre-enabled. The list mixes working skills with a broken one and a sandbox API — which is a governance problem as much as a usability one, since a user has no way to tell which is which.

What changed

A catalogue rather than a settings list: what each skill does, what it needs, what to say to invoke it, and whether it is ready to use or waiting on a connection. Broken and UAT entries do not belong in a production workspace at all.

Why it matters: the skills are the value on top of the model, and today they are presented as engineering configuration. Naming them in the user's language turns an 18-row settings screen into a list of things the customer can already do.

12

It works on a phone

Addendum §2High

What was wrong

The live build assumes a desktop. On a phone it is unusable — which matters for a product whose users are on sites, in vehicles and in meetings rather than at a desk for most of their working day.

What changed

All six prototypes were built and checked at 320, 375, 414, 768, 1024 and 1440 px with no horizontal page overflow at any width. Tables, code blocks, diagrams and maths scroll inside their own containers rather than moving the page. Keyboard-shortcut hints hide on touch widths.

Why it matters: a delivery lead checking a slipped purchase order does it from a site office on a phone, not from a desk. Today they cannot.

§4

The six prototypes

Six complete, interactive, single-file builds — light and dark, working keyboard and mouse, every fix above already in. They are six different products, not six skins: the point is to choose a direction. All data is local and invented; nothing is transmitted anywhere.

Familiarapp 1

The ChatGPT idiom — neutral, low-chrome, zero learning curve. The model picker at the top of the transcript is what replaces the deleted global header.

apps/app-1-chatgpt.html Consideredapp 2

Warm paper light mode, editorial rhythm. Documents, diagrams and code open in a split artifact panel beside the chat, with Preview and Code tabs.

apps/app-2-claude.html Provableapp 3

Sovereignty as the primary UI. Click any answer and its residency receipt loads — node, egress bytes, PII masking, retention, signed hash.

apps/app-3-sovereign.html Readyapp 4

The home screen is the product. Intent cards draft an editable brief instead of firing a question the user never wrote.

apps/app-4-launchpad.html Groundedapp 5

Provenance first. The 16-source Agentic Twin graph moves from a 12 px screen-edge tab into a context rail with a live coverage meter.

apps/app-5-twin.html Horizonapp 6

A wide operational canvas. Answers, artifacts and diagrams sit side by side, each message ending in the same anchored action row.
