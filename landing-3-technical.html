§01  Architecture

Where a prompt goes, and where it stops

Everything below the dashed line runs inside your network boundary. The workspace, the policy engine, the audit log, the model weights, the GPUs they run on, the retrieval index and the connector plane are all in-tenancy. Public model APIs are not reachable from the inference tier — not by configuration convention, but because the route does not exist.

↔ Diagram scrolls horizontally · the numbered path is written out below

In-networkStays inside the boundary. 0 bytes to the public internet.
Egress by designSigned model artefacts inbound at install; outbound mail on your own transport.
No routeDenied at the policy engine and absent from the perimeter route table.
BoundaryYour network. Everything inside it is your tenancy, your keys, your logs.
  1. 1

    Prompt

    Browser to workspace over TLS 1.3 with mutual auth and OIDC identity. Device posture is checked before the session opens.

  2. 2

    Policy, then route

    The policy engine stamps the turn's sovereignty tier. On PRIVATE, egress is deny-all and the router may only select an on-POD model.

  3. 3

    Inference and tool call

    The model runs on your GPUs. If it needs SAP, P6, Jira or mail, it calls them through the MCP gateway — inside the boundary.

  4. 4

    Result returns

    Tool output and retrieved context come back to the model. Endpoint, verb and a payload hash are written to the audit log.

  5. 5

    Response

    Streamed to the operator on the same session. The audit record closes before the answer is released. Public internet byte count: 0.

The claim, stated so it can be falsified

Put a span port on the uplink and run a private-tier turn. You will see traffic between the workspace tier, the POD and your systems of record. You will see no session to a public model endpoint, because the inference tier has no default route and no DNS resolver that answers for one. That is the test we ask you to run during the walkthrough.

§02  Data flow & residency

Per operation, what leaves

The honest version. Five of the seven operations below produce zero bytes of egress. Two of them cross the boundary, and we would rather you read about them here than find them in a packet capture: inbound signed model artefacts at install time, and outbound mail, which leaves on the mail transport you already run.

Table 1 — Data flow and residency, private tier
Operation Terminates at Leaves your network Bytes to public internet Where it rests Audit record
Prompt submission Workspace tier — session orchestrator In-network 0 Session store, in-tenancy Yes — actor, tier, thread
Model inference MagnaREACTOR POD — 8×H200 In-network 0 KV cache for the turn only; weights resident on the POD Yes — model, tokens in/out
Retrieval Digital-twin context graph, in-tenancy index In-network 0 Your index, your storage, your encryption keys Yes — documents cited
Tool call — SAP, P6, Jira MCP gateway → your systems of record In-network 0 Nothing new; the source system remains the record Yes — endpoint, verb, payload hash
Mail send via MCP Your existing mail transport — Exchange or M365 Egress by design = your current mail path Your mail system, under your retention policy Yes — recipient, subject, message-id
Platform telemetry In-tenancy health agent Opt-in only 0 by default In-tenancy. If enabled: aggregate counters, never payloads Yes — export events
Model artefacts Signed registry → POD, install time Inbound, signed 0 at run time Verified by digest, resident on the POD, offline thereafter Yes — digest, signer, operator
Public model APIs — No route n/a Not reachable from the inference tier Attempt is logged and denied

↔ Table scrolls horizontally

  • †Figures describe the private tier. MagnaVERSE also exposes higher tiers for work that is explicitly cleared to use external services; those tiers are opt-in per workspace, gated by a confirmation the operator has to give, and marked in the transcript and the audit log.
  • ‡“Bytes to public internet” counts payload and control traffic originated by MagnaVERSE. It does not include traffic your own systems generate independently.
Why the mail row is not zero

A mail send is not an AI operation — it is a mail operation. When an agent drafts a note to a subcontractor and you approve it, the message leaves through your Exchange or M365 transport, hits your DLP rules and lands in your journal, exactly as it would if a person had pressed send. MagnaVERSE never opens its own path to the internet to deliver it.

Air-gapped variant

Where even install-time fetch is unacceptable, model artefacts are delivered on physical media, verified against a published digest by your team, and loaded on a POD with no WAN interface at all. That configuration produces a residency table with no amber rows.

§04  Model roster

Four models, all resident on your POD

Weights live on the tenancy. The router selects among them per turn under policy; it cannot fall back to anything off-POD, which is the failure mode that makes most “private AI” deployments leak. If the selected model is unavailable, the turn fails loudly rather than quietly going somewhere else.

§05  Deployment topologies

Three ways to run it, compared honestly

The trade is always the same: the more control you hold, the more operational burden you carry. Data residency is guaranteed in-country in all three; what changes is the network path, the key custody and who gets paged at 03:00.

Table 4 — Deployment topologies
Attribute On-premises Sovereign cloud Edge
Where inference runs Your data centre, your racks, your floor space Magna-operated in-country region At the site — plant, yard, rig, project office
Network path, workspace → GPU Same L2/L3 domain. No WAN hop. One private interconnect hop. No public transit. Site LAN. No dependency on the core.
Latency profile Lowest bounded by the fabric, not the network Low adds the interconnect RTT Lowest, locally smaller models, closer
Data residency In your building In-country, contractually and physically At the site where the data is produced
Key custody Your HSM / KMS. Magna never holds them. Your keys — BYOK, or an HSM you control in-region Sealed appliance keys, escrowed to you
Control plane In-tenancy In-region Local; reconciles to the core when the link is up
Operational burden on your team High facilities, power, cooling, lifecycle — or hand it to 24/7 managed ops Low Magna runs it, 24/7 Shared you host, Magna manages remotely
Scale unit POD, or POD slice Tenancy — e.g. 8×H200 Edge node
Disconnected operation Full survives WAN loss indefinitely Interconnect-dependent Full designed for intermittent links
Best fit Regulated data that is not permitted to leave the premises In-country residency without the capital expenditure Sites with poor, expensive or hostile connectivity

↔ Table scrolls horizontally

Mixed estates are the normal case

Most deployments are not one column. A sovereign-cloud tenancy carries the head-office workspace, an on-prem POD carries the regulated workload, and edge nodes sit at the sites that lose their link every time the weather turns. One control plane, one policy model, one audit trail across all three.

§07  Governance & compliance

Provable, not promised

Sovereignty that cannot be evidenced is marketing. Every turn produces an audit record before its answer is released, and the record says which model ran, on which tenancy, under which tier, against which documents, and what it wrote where.

What the CISO gets to keep

Your identity provider stays authoritative. Your keys stay in your custody. Your DLP still inspects mail. Your SIEM ingests the audit stream. MagnaVERSE adds a capability; it does not ask you to move a control out of your estate to get it.

What it does not do

It does not train on your data. It does not send prompts or completions to a model vendor. It does not phone home to make a routing decision. If any of those become true for a tier you enable deliberately, the transcript says so at the point of use.
